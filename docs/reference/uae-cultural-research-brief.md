## PART 1. EMIRATI FACIAL FEATURES

### Primary Source
**"Face it, Emiratis are just better at recognising people"**
The National / Research by Dr. Susanne Quadflieg, University of Bristol
Study conducted with 170 students (Emirati and American), testing face recognition across eyes, nose, and mouth regions.

**Key findings for character design:**

The nose is the most culturally significant facial feature in Emirati identity. In Emirati poetry and everyday language, the ideal nose is described as *sallat al saif*, "like a drawn sword." Defining characteristics:
- Straight bridge (no bump, no concavity)
- Narrow nostrils
- Pointed tip

This is not an aesthetic preference. It is a cultural identity marker embedded in poetry, language, and greeting customs (the traditional nose-rub greeting between close friends and family). The study found that Emiratis are specifically superior at identifying faces by nose shape, a skill attributed to lifelong experience with faces framed by head coverings that obscure other features.

**Practical implication:** The *sallat al saif* nose is the single feature most likely to "trigger" Emirati recognition in a viewer. It must be present, simplified, and amplified to read at the game's stylization level.

### Secondary Source
**Document: "5 Physical Features and 5 Cultural Elements of the UAE"**
Scribd educational document.

Documented physical features common among Emiratis:
- Oval or round face shape
- Large almond-shaped eyes

**Practical implication:** Round-to-oval face shape (not angular). Large almond eyes, meaning elongated with a defined outer corner, not wide-set round eyes.

### Tertiary Source
**"Genome Wide Association Study reveals novel associations with face morphology in the UAE population"**
NCBI / PubMed Central. Open Access
DOI available at: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11809905/

Study conducted with 172 Emirati participants (89 male, 83 female), ages 18–49. Used 3D facial scanning and automatic landmarking to obtain 44 linear and angular face measurements. First GWAS study on face morphology in a Middle Eastern population.

Note: This is a genetic study. It does not provide a simple list of visual traits, but confirms that Emirati facial morphology has measurable specific characteristics distinct from other MENA populations. Useful as academic backing when discussing the project.

## PART 2. SKIN TONE

### Source: Daniel's Direct Observation (E-Line Media Creative Director)
Confirmed through in-person experience in the UAE:

- Local Emirati women have a paler complexion than commonly expected for the MENA region
- Not super pale
- Not yellowed or darkened as seen in other ethnic groups in the region
- Yellow tones exist in the skin but must not dominate
- This is a real visual distinction, not a Western assumption

**Practical implication:** Skin tone pass should target a warm light-medium range. Pull back yellow saturation significantly. Do not push toward the darker MENA tones used for other characters.

## PART 3. TRADITIONAL DRESS AND CULTURAL MARKERS

### Primary Source
**"What you need to know about Emirati national dress"**
Gulf News

**Burqa (pronounced "burga" locally):**
A rigid face mask worn over the nose, mouth, and chin. Made from shiny golden or metallic fabric. Shows only the eyes and cheeks. Traditionally said to imitate the facial features of a falcon. It protects the face from desert sun and sand. The most iconic visual marker of traditional Emirati women.

**Abuteela / Jalabiya / Women's Kandura:**
The traditional dress. Colors are rich and varied: deep greens, reds, whites, mustard golds. Decorated with:
- Embroidery along neckline, sleeves, and hem (silver or gold thread)
- Floral and geometric patterns
- Sequins and gems in more formal versions
- "Abu Al Tyoor" fabric (flowers with bird-wing-shaped leaves) used for special occasions and bride's dresses

**Shailah:**
Wide black scarf worn over the head, covering hair. Older and more conservative women wear it tightly; younger women more loosely.

**Henna:**
Traditional hand decoration. Classic pattern: circle motif in the palms and coloring at the fingertips.

## PART 4. EMIRATI ANIMATION: FREEJ AS DESIGN REFERENCE

### Primary Source
**"FREEJ Character Development"**
Past Forward / Meridian International Center

Freej (2006). Created by Mohammed Saeed Harib. First 3D animated series in the Middle East. Cultural icon throughout the Gulf region. Four elderly Emirati women protagonists: Umm Saeed, Umm Saloom, Umm Allawi, Umm Khammas.

**Why Freej is the direct design reference for the Old Female variant:**
- Only existing 3D animated production featuring elderly traditional Emirati women
- Creator conducted extensive research into Emirati poetry, dialect, and cultural heritage
- Solved the exact same design problem: how to make traditional Emirati elderly women readable and appealing in 3D stylized animation
- Has been a cultural icon for 18+ years, meaning it passed the "recognition" test with actual Emirati audiences

**What to study in Freej:**
- How the burqa shape was simplified into 3D stylized geometry
- How limited facial real estate (mostly eyes above the burqa) carries personality and emotion
- How traditional dress texture and color translates to game-ready stylized assets
- How the Abuteela dress silhouette reads from a distance

**Design approach of Mohammed Saeed Harib (creator):**
"Protecting Emirati culture was the paramount objective. I conducted extensive research to reintroduce old Emirati poetry and phrases that are slowly falling out of fashion."
Source: Sultan Al Qassemi / Arab News interview.

## PART 5. UAE ANIMATION INDUSTRY CONTEXT

### Source
**"The UAE animation industry: Current perceptions, challenges and future directions"**
Tariq & Willard. Animation Practice, Process & Production, Vol. 11, Issue 1, 2022, pp. 31–54.
Publisher: Intellect. DOI: 10.1386/ap3_00033_1

**Key findings:**
- Japanese anime (dubbed in Arabic) was the dominant animated content in Emirati households from the 1990s onward
- Emirati youth grew up consuming primarily foreign animated content
- Local Emirati animation is now actively trying to respond to audiences shaped by anime and American cartoons
- The challenge for UAE animation: meeting the visual quality expectations set by international productions while representing local culture authentically

**Practical implication for character design:**
Emirati audiences are visually sophisticated and have high expectations set by anime and Disney/Pixar. A stylized character that reads as "generic Western cartoon" will not satisfy this audience. The cultural markers must be present AND the production quality must be high.

---

### Source
**"Cross-Cultural Narratives in Animation: A Comparative Analysis"**
International Journal of Research and Innovation in Social Science, 2025.
https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/articles/cross-cultural-narratives-in-animation-a-comparative-analysis-of-eastern-and-western-representations-and-their-impact-on-cultural-integrity/

**Directly relevant finding:**
Uses Moana as a case study for how animation can preserve cultural identity in the context of global distribution. The research identifies that authentic cultural representation in animation promotes cultural awareness and appreciation, while distortion of cultural markers, even in well-intentioned productions, undermines both the work and the community it represents.

**The Moana parallel (key for this project):**
Moana succeeded because Polynesian features were stylized WITHIN Disney's visual language, not replaced by it. The specific ethnic markers (face shape, skin tone, hair texture) were simplified and amplified to read at Disney's stylization level. This is the exact model to follow for Emirati characters in Endstar.

## PART 6. QUICK REFERENCE CHECKLIST FOR EMIRATI FACE

Use this checklist when evaluating any Emirati character WIP:

**Nose (highest priority)**
☐ Straight bridge, no bump
☐ Narrow nostrils, not flared
☐ Pointed tip
☐ Nose reads as *sallat al saif* when simplified to game stylization level

**Eyes**
☐ Almond shape, elongated, not round
☐ Defined outer corner
☐ Socket depth visible, eyes are not flush against lid surface
☐ Eyelid rim (lip of the eyelid) present and constructed
☐ Lacrimal caruncle area defined

**Face shape**
☐ Oval to round, not angular or square
☐ Jaw does not dominate the silhouette
☐ Cheekbones present but not exaggerated

**Skin tone**
☐ Warm light-medium range
☐ Yellow not dominant
☐ Not pushed toward darker MENA tones

**Stylization check**
☐ Face reads at same stylization level as approved UAE Female reference character
☐ Not more realistic than the approved character
☐ Disney cranium-to-face ratio maintained
☐ Emotional readability from distance
