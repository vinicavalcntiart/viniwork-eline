# DCT feedback on the female characters

**Source:** Department of Culture and Tourism, Abu Dhabi. Dated 28 Sep 2026.
Three items, all priority 5 High, all "Not Started" as of 30 Sep.

This is the Emirati read the whole project was built to pass. It arrived on the
delivered females.

---

## The three items, verbatim

**Row of 4, burqa and abaya.** "Burga is in dark gold/brass color only, no silver.
The abaya on the far left is modern, should be the draped one. Long necklace on far
right character should be in gold color, silver was worn but the style is different.
The short necklace is not correct and would be hidden by the head scarf."

**Row of 4, dress border and jewellery.** "The burga doesn't come in silver, please
change. The design of the women's dress bottom border isn't Emirati, I've attached
images for reference. Silver jewelry isn't correct style, anything close to the neck
would be hidden by the headscarf, maybe make the necklaces longer."

**Last row, 3 characters.** "This style is not Emirati, please use the styles with
comments above, also reference: Zuhba, Brides Procession" (Abu Dhabi Culture
heritage register).

---

## What it confirms

Every locked decision that the DCT touched, it confirmed. Burqa in dark gold or
brass: locked in the Manual. Jewellery in dark aged gold: locked. The notes land on
silver burqa, silver jewellery, a modern abaya, a non-Emirati dress border. Those
are deviations from the Manual, not the Manual.

The dress border note is the one to hold onto. The elder's underdress carried the
Al Sadu band pattern, registered as `[EXTENSION]` because Al Sadu is tent and camel
textile rather than garment. If that is the border the DCT rejected, the one element
the project marked as beyond its evidence is the one element the cultural authority
flagged. The extension flag did what it was for. This goes in the article's
Discussion and in the Manual's next revision.

Which characters sit in the "row of 4" and the "last row of 3" is not visible from
the sheet. Some may be texture variants, some may be other artists' work. Sort by
character before assigning.

## The two references, read

Both are bridal and wedding practices. That is the register the DCT is pointing at
for "Emirati style".

**Zuhba.** The bridal trousseau the groom's family provides: gold, clothes, perfumes.
Clothes named: sheila, burqa, silk abaya (suwai'a), kandora, abu badla trousers.
Fabrics: mazraiah or mukhawir, with named varieties (Abu Nira, Abu Tira, Abu Qalim,
Al Sultani, Al Braissem, Al Sawari). Gold ornaments named: shanaf, tassa, kawashi,
mourta'asha, marriyah, huyool, shawahid, marami.

**Brides Procession.** The bride wears "a thawb, a kandoura, and a mazray, typically
in shades of green or blue", with bou badla trousers and a burqa. In the Al Ain
oases, an indigo (neel) burqa and indigo kandoura. Jewellery: murtaisha, kawashi,
rings. Henna on hands and feet, saffron on the cheeks.

**What the two pages give the fix, in plain terms:**

- Layered dress: a sheer overdress on top of the long dress, not one garment.
- Colour register for occasion wear: green or blue.
- Jewellery: gold, long, sitting below the scarf. Named pieces are documented if
  the Manual wants to cite them.
- Burqa: the indigo cloth variant is documented alongside the gold. Both exist.
  The DCT still says gold or brass for these characters.
- Neither page describes the dress border pattern visually. The DCT's attached
  images are the only reference for that.
