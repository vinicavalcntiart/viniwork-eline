## 11 Sep 2026 · Male, Young · dressed bust · Daniel's notes

**Daniel:** "Couple things: more rounded beard, he's giving System of a Down vibes
with that beard (great band though). On the Kandura, I can't overstate enough how
there should be no collar or embroidery. It is a very understated and flattened
design. Emirati Kanduras have much less fancy elements, the most fancy thing you
will mostly see on there is the [tassel]. That pleated pattern that makes a
triangle going down also extends to the shoulders and meets at the seam (most of
the time)."

Three files: a photo of a man in a plain white robe, a close-up of the tassel and
placket stitching, and the WIP marked in red.

**No note on the face.** Nose, neck, age, eyes: nothing. The previous round is
closed.

**What the red marks say**

- Beard: a rounded U following the jaw, no point at the chin. The WIP beard
  narrows to a triangle under the chin and the moustache sits separate, which is
  the pointed read Daniel is naming. Rounder also reads younger.
- Robe neckline: the raised band around the neck and the embroidered edge are
  crossed out. The real garment has a flat round-cut neckline with no standing
  band at all, a short buttoned opening with small fabric-covered buttons, and the
  tassel hanging from a loop at the top button.
- Robe pattern: the only decoration is top-stitching, not embroidery. Several
  parallel rows of stitching run vertically along the placket and converge into a
  point below it. From the neckline, stitched seam lines run diagonally out to the
  shoulder seam on each side, which is the "triangle" that "extends to the
  shoulders". Daniel drew those lines in red from the neck opening to the
  shoulders.

**Correction to earlier direction.** The brief said the robe "can be elegantly and
subtly embroidered" on Khalaf's wording and suggested tone-on-tone embroidery at
the neckline. Khalaf says "can be", meaning it exists, not that it is the default.
Daniel's in-person read is that the default is flat and plain, with stitching as
the only surface detail. Daniel's observation wins on production, the same way it
did for skin tone. The embroidery note was a misread of evidence weight and is
withdrawn.

**Pipeline reading.** With no spec map and all-white albedo, the stitched seam
lines are the material read for the robe. They go into the normal map, with a
touch of roughness variation in the mixmap, and they also solve the white-on-white
separation that would otherwise be a problem.

## 1 Sep 2026 · Male, Young · head blockout, three views

**Daniel:** "nice, looking a bit too disney in some cases perhaps? also just from the
bust he looks like he'd be more of a lifter than someone who does farming, maybe it's

**Read of the WIP against the locked decisions**

Working: eye construction (rim built, socket depth, inner corner defined), skin tone
inside the range, oval face with cheekbones present and not exaggerated, beard and hair
as clean graphic masses, strong silhouette in all three views.

Nose: bridge is straight, which is right. Tip is a short rounded ball, level or
slightly up in profile, nostril base on the wide side. That is the generic stylized
nose, and it is what reads as Disney. The locked profile is a longer dorsum, pointed
tip, descending, narrow base. Amplifying the nose also shifts the eye-to-nose ratio,
which pulls the eyes out of the Disney read without touching them.

Neck: width close to the jaw width, both neck muscles ropey and separate, trapezius
rising high. Reads as a lifter, as Daniel says. Face itself is lean, so the mismatch
is in the neck, not the head.

**Priority:** nose first, since it is the cultural marker and the Disney fix at once.
Neck second, quick Move-brush pass.
